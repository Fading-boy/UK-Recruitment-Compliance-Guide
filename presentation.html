UK Recruitment Guide

Compliance Processing

For Senior Recruitment Managers

1. Why Compliance Matters

Business Objective
  • Protect the company from legal risk
  • Prevent fraud and misrepresentation
  • Safeguard vulnerable people and clients
  • Maintain a defensible recruitment process
  • Ensure the right person is hired for the right role
What Must Be Verified
  • Identity verification
  • Right to Work + visa status
  • DBS + overseas police checks
  • Occupational Health clearance
  • References and gap checks
  • Professional registration
Result-oriented approach: compliance is a legal and operational safeguard.

2. Identity Verification

Purpose
  • Confirm identity is genuine
  • Prevent fraud or impersonation
  • Match candidate to official documents
Examples of Acceptable Documents
  • Passport
  • Driving licence + proof of address
  • Birth certificate + NI number
  • EU/EEA ID card
Request original documents
Check validity and photo
Match DOB and name
Certify copy and file
Record verification
Always verify originals in person where possible and keep a clear audit trail.

3. Right to Work + Visa Sponsorship

Key Rule

You must check a candidate's legal right to work in the UK before they start employment.

  • British / EEA / Swiss citizen: usually straightforward
  • Non-EEA with valid visa: check expiry and restrictions
  • Non-EEA with no visa: sponsorship required
Gather nationality and visa status
Review passport and visa details
Check work restrictions
No visa? Discuss sponsorship
If sponsoring is required, the role cannot legally start until sponsorship is approved.

4. DBS + Overseas Police Checks

Level Use Case What It Covers
Basic General roles Unspent convictions only
Standard Regulatory or higher-trust roles Spent + unspent convictions and cautions
Enhanced Child or vulnerable adult roles Full police record + local info
Enhanced + Barred List Safeguarding jobs Enhanced check + barred list review
Overseas police checks are required when a candidate has lived or worked abroad for 6+ months within the relevant period.

5. Occupational Health Clearance

Purpose
  • Confirm fitness for the role
  • Protect employee wellbeing and safety
  • Identify reasonable adjustments
Important Principle
  • Occupational Health should usually be post-offer
  • Do not ask inappropriate health questions at interview
  • Reasonable adjustments should be considered
Issue conditional offer
Send OH questionnaire
Review specialist report
Arrange adjustments if needed

6. References and Employment Gap Checks

Reference Rule

Always seek references from previous employers during the last 3 years and investigate any employment gaps.

  • Prefer direct manager or senior referee
  • Verify dates, job title, and employment status
  • Ask about performance and reasons for leaving
  • Check if the reference says they would rehire
  • Investigate gaps with clear explanations
Unexplained gaps or conflicting dates are major red flags requiring further investigation.

7. Professional Registration

Why It Matters
  • Required in regulated professions
  • Protects public safety
  • Ensures legal compliance
  • Prevents hiring unqualified workers
Profession Typical Regulator
DoctorGMC
NurseNMC
SolicitorLaw Society
TeacherTRA
AccountantICAEW / ACCA / ICAS
Never rely on a candidate's copy alone. Verify registration directly with the official regulator.

Critical Rule

If any item is missing, unclear, or contradictory, do not shortcut the process. Investigate further and document the decision.

9. Key Takeaways for Senior Recruitment Managers

  • Compliance is a legal and operational safeguard
  • Always verify identity and right to work before the start date
  • Different roles require different DBS and regulatory checks
  • Record all checks and keep secure audit trails
  • Investigate gaps, conflicting dates, and references carefully
  • Use reasonable adjustments rather than rejecting automatically
  • Professional registration must be verified directly and independently
  • Document decisions in case of later challenge or audit
Simple rule: if there is doubt, investigate, document, and escalate based on risk and policy.
